We often long to remain in the quiet threshold of childhood, where objects hum with secret logic and reality slips gently out of frame. A table just out of reach, steam from something that shouldn’t breathe, letters dissipating beneath a veil—these are not dreams, but subtle distortions that linger under waking life. They speak a language we half-remember: shaped not by clarity, but by intuition, tension, and rupture. In times of fracture—political and emotional—such misalignments don’t just soothe; they offer another kind of truth. Not one we explain, but one we enter. A language beyond words, where misunderstanding becomes closeness, and strangeness, a kind of shelter. When the present becomes unspeakable, it’s often the gestures, textures, and quiet dissonances that remain. What surrealism once offered wasn’t chaos, but a reordering of sense—a belief in meaning beyond logic. Childhood, too, is not a place we return to, but a flickering tool: one that scatters what’s solid, so something true might briefly glimmer in the fragments.
